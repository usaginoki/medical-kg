# USDA's FoodData Central: what is it and why is it needed today?

> [!abstract] TL;DR
> Perspective paper from USDA-ARS describing FoodData Central (FDC), the integrated web system that replaced the Standard
> Reference database: five data types in one place, with Foundation Foods and Experimental Foods as the new, metadata-rich
> "bridge to the future".

## What was built
- **Dataset(s):** [[USDA FoodData Central]]
- **Sources & construction:** USDA's >100 years of food-composition work, reorganised into five data types: Foundation Foods
  (FF), Experimental Foods (EF), Standard Reference (SR) Legacy, Food and Nutrient Database for Dietary Studies (FNDDS),
  and the Global Branded Food Products Database (GBFPD).
- **Size & coverage:** US food supply. (In our download: 469 Foundation foods in the 2026-04-30 release and 7,793 SR Legacy foods.)
- **Evaluation / applications:** public health, agricultural/environmental research, policy, clinical nutrition, product development.

## Key findings
Only the abstract was read (article not open access; no PMC copy):
1. FDC provides 5 distinct data types in one place, each with its own purpose.
2. Foundation Foods and Experimental Foods provide data and metadata "never previously available from a database" (sample-level values, methods, provenance).
3. SR Legacy, FNDDS and branded data remain as the familiar established types.
4. The change was motivated by the growing number and variety of foods, new analytical methods and new agricultural practices.

## Relevance to research questions
### Q2: Cultural ingredient datasets
FDC is the nutrient backbone most national food-composition tables crosswalk to. It is the practical hub for mapping
ingredients from cultural recipe datasets to nutrients, and its Foundation metadata (FoodOn, NCBI Taxon) links to compound
databases (FooDB). It does not itself describe non-US cultures.

See [[Q2 Cultural ingredient datasets]]

## Limitations / caveats
- Full text paywalled; this note is based on the abstract and the data themselves.
- US-only food supply; Foundation Foods coverage is still small.
